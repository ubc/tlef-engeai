Welcome to Scenario Generation

Build a bank of practice scenarios your students can work through

Practice scenarios, drafted by AI and approved by you

Scenario Generation helps you build a practice question bank without writing every scenario from scratch. You describe the situation you want students to reason about, and the assistant drafts a full scenario with sub-questions and a worked solution for you to review.

What you can do:

  • Draft a scenario from a short prompt and your learning objectives
  • Choose which sub-question types appear and how difficult the scenario is
  • Edit any part of a draft before students ever see it
  • Publish scenarios individually, on your own schedule

Nothing in this tutorial is real. The scenario you generate on the next step is a fixed example. No scenario is saved to your course and no AI model is called.

Diagnosing Heat Exchanger Fouling

Scenario. Six months into service, a shell-and-tube exchanger on your cooling loop shows an 8 °C drop in cooling water outlet temperature at unchanged flow rates. Inlet conditions and pump curves are unchanged.

Sub-questions.

  • Conceptual. Name two physical mechanisms that would reduce overall heat transfer without any change in flow rate.
  • Calculation. Using the given duty and temperatures, estimate the current overall heat transfer coefficient and compare it to the clean value.
  • Interpretation. Based on your comparison, argue whether cleaning or retubing is the appropriate intervention.

This draft is visible only to you. Students cannot see it until you publish it.

Reviewing and Publishing Drafts

You decide what students see, and when

Every generated scenario starts as a draft

A draft is not a hidden question — it does not exist for students at all. Requesting one directly returns a not-found response rather than a permission error, so students cannot even learn that unpublished scenarios exist.

  • Review the scenario body. Check that the situation is solvable with what your students know.
  • Review each sub-question. Add, remove, reorder, or rewrite any of them.
  • Review the worked solution. It is never shown to students while they attempt the scenario.
  • Check the estimated time. It is derived from the difficulty you chose and is worth adjusting.

Generating in batches. You can request several scenarios at once, up to ten. Siblings from one batch are grouped so you can review them together and publish only the ones that earn it.

The assistant drafts; you decide. A generated scenario is a starting point, not an approved assessment. Nothing reaches your students without you publishing it.

Welcome to Scenario Generation!

Scenario Generation drafts practice scenarios for your course from a short prompt, so you can build a question bank by reviewing and editing rather than writing from a blank page.

What you'll learn:

  • How to describe a scenario you want drafted
  • How drafts differ from published scenarios
  • What to check before publishing

Generating a scenario

The form takes a base question or situation, the learning objectives it should target, which sub-question types to include, and a difficulty level.

Press Generate to see an example draft. In your real course this calls the assistant; here it reveals a fixed example, so nothing is saved and no model runs.

Tip: a specific prompt with concrete numbers produces a far more useful draft than a broad topic name.

Reviewing drafts

Every generated scenario is a draft. Students cannot see drafts at all — not as locked items, not as previews.

Before publishing, check that the scenario is solvable with what your students currently know, that each sub-question is answerable, and that the worked solution is correct.

You can generate up to ten scenarios in one batch and publish only the ones you want.
