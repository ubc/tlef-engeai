Welcome to Writing Feedback

Draft formative feedback on student writing, with you approving every word that reaches a student

Feedback that quotes the student's own writing

Writing Feedback reads a verified student submission against a rubric you approved, and drafts formative comments anchored to exact passages in that submission. It does not rewrite the student's sentences and it does not produce a model answer.

How writing gets in:

  • Create an assignment directly, giving it a title and optional due date.
  • Or import one from your LMS, bringing its description along with it.
  • Add submissions as pasted text or uploaded documents.
  • Verify the text before anything is generated — staff confirm the extracted text matches what the student actually wrote.

Verification is not optional. Feedback is generated only from staff-verified text, because every quotation in the feedback must exist exactly in the student's submission.

Nothing in this tutorial is real. Every example below is fixed sample content. No assignment, rubric, submission, or feedback is created in your course.

Building and Approving a Rubric

Each assignment carries its own rubric, and feedback waits until you approve it

A rubric describes the assignment, not just its scoring

Alongside criteria and levels, a rubric records the task, the audience, the purpose, the constraints students must work within, the learning outcomes it assesses, and your grading intent. That context is what keeps drafted feedback aimed at your assignment rather than at generic "good writing".

Criteria

Between 1 and 10. Each has a label and a description.

  • Organization — structure, staging, and cohesion across the report
  • Content — accuracy and sufficiency of technical explanation
  • Sources — attribution, citation, and integration of evidence

Performance levels

Between 2 and 8, ranked contiguously from 1.

  1. Weak
  2. Developing
  3. Proficient
  4. Exemplary

Points are all-or-nothing. Give every level a point value or leave every level ordinal. A partial scale would let a numeric grade be invented from levels that never had one, so it is rejected.

Saving a draft is not approving it. The two are deliberately separate actions. Feedback generation stays blocked until an approved rubric exists.

After the first approval, structure is frozen. Labels, descriptions, ordering, and points stay editable in later versions, but the set of criteria and levels stops changing, so feedback already stored against them cannot be silently reinterpreted.

Reviewing Generated Feedback

Every comment points at an exact passage you can check

Generated feedback arrives as anchored comments. Each one names the criterion it addresses and quotes the passage it is about, so you can verify the judgement rather than take it on trust.

Organization Developing
"The results are shown in Figure 2. The heat transfer coefficient dropped. We also measured pressure drop."

Three findings are presented in sequence without stating how they relate. Consider signalling which observation is the main result and which support it, so the reader knows what the section is arguing.

Sources Proficient
"Fouling resistance typically increases asymptotically over the first year of service (Kern, 1965)."

The claim is attributed and the citation supports the specific point being made. This is the pattern to repeat in the discussion section, where several claims are currently unattributed.

Quotations are verified, not paraphrased. A comment can only quote text that appears exactly in the verified submission. If the assistant cannot ground a judgement in the student's own words, it does not make it.

Edit anything. Rewrite a comment, delete one you disagree with, or add your own. Your revisions are what the student sees.

Approving and Releasing

Two separate decisions, both yours

Approving marks the feedback as finished and ready. Releasing is what actually delivers it. Keeping them apart means you can finish reviewing a stack of submissions and still choose when students receive them.

This example submission has been reviewed and its comments edited. Approve it to continue.

Approved

The feedback is now marked approved. In your real course the release card appears next, showing exactly what the student would receive, with a preview before anything is sent.

  • Students never see confidence scores, internal flags, model details, or staff-only notes.
  • Release is blocked while a rubric has changed since generation, so a student cannot receive feedback judged against a rubric that no longer exists.
  • Nothing is delivered automatically. Release is always an action you take.
