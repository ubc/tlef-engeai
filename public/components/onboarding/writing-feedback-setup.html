Building and Approving a Rubric

Each assignment carries its own rubric, and feedback waits until you approve it

Give the feedback enough context

The rubric combines the assignment's purpose and learning outcomes with the criteria and performance levels course staff will use.

Criteria

Name what staff should look for.

  • Organization — structure, staging, and cohesion across the report
  • Content — accuracy and sufficiency of technical explanation
  • Sources — attribution, citation, and integration of evidence

Performance levels

Describe how performance differs.

  1. Weak
  2. Developing
  3. Proficient
  4. Exemplary

Saving a draft is not approving it. The two are deliberately separate actions. Feedback generation stays blocked until an approved rubric exists.

Course staff can keep editing. Rubric versions protect feedback already generated against an earlier approved version.

Reviewing Generated Feedback

Every comment points at an exact passage you can check

Each draft comment names its criterion and quotes the passage it addresses, so course staff can check the evidence before editing or approving it.

Organization Developing
"The results are shown in Figure 2. The heat transfer coefficient dropped. We also measured pressure drop."

Three findings are presented in sequence without stating how they relate. Consider signalling which observation is the main result and which support it, so the reader knows what the section is arguing.

Sources Proficient
"Fouling resistance typically increases asymptotically over the first year of service (Kern, 1965)."

The claim is attributed and the citation supports the specific point being made. This is the pattern to repeat in the discussion section, where several claims are currently unattributed.

Quotations are verified, not paraphrased. A comment can only quote text that appears exactly in the verified submission. If the assistant cannot ground a judgement in the student's own words, it does not make it.

Edit anything. Rewrite, remove, or add comments. The reviewed version is what the student sees.

Approving and Releasing

Two separate decisions, both yours

Approving marks feedback as reviewed. Releasing delivers it. Keeping them separate lets course staff control timing.

This example submission has been reviewed and its comments edited. Approve it to continue.

Approved

The feedback is now marked approved. In the full workspace, a release preview shows exactly what the student would receive.

  • Students never see confidence scores, internal flags, model details, or staff-only notes.
  • Release is blocked while a rubric has changed since generation, so a student cannot receive feedback judged against a rubric that no longer exists.
  • Nothing is delivered automatically. Course staff must choose Release.

Welcome to Writing Feedback!

Writing Feedback drafts formative comments on student writing, anchored to exact passages and judged against a rubric you approved.

Getting writing in: import an assignment and submissions from Canvas, or create an assignment manually and add submissions as text or documents.

Verification: staff confirm the extracted text matches the student's actual writing before any feedback is generated. This is what makes exact quotation possible.

Rubrics

Each assignment has its own rubric, holding the task, audience, purpose, constraints, learning outcomes, and grading intent alongside its criteria and levels.

Limits: 1 to 10 criteria, and 2 to 8 performance levels ranked contiguously from 1.

Points: either every level has points or none does. Partial scales are rejected because they would let a numeric grade be invented.

Approval: saving a draft and approving it are separate actions. Generation stays blocked until an approved rubric exists.

Later edits: after first approval, labels, descriptions, order, and points remain editable. The criteria and level sets stay stable so stored feedback is not silently reinterpreted.

Reviewing feedback

Each comment names its criterion and quotes the exact passage it addresses. Quotations must appear verbatim in the verified submission — the assistant will not make a judgement it cannot ground in the student's own words.

You can rewrite, delete, or add comments. Your edited version is what the student receives.

Approval and release

Approving marks feedback as finished. Releasing delivers it. They are separate so you can control timing.

Press Approve to continue. Nothing is saved — this is a fixed example.
